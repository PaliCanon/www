The Tipiṭaka, the "three baskets", is the oldest record of the Buddha's teaching: what he taught more than twenty-five centuries ago, recited by his disciples and written down in Pāli in Sri Lanka in the first century BCE.

At its heart are the Four Noble Truths, the Buddha's first teaching: there is suffering; suffering arises from craving; suffering can end; and the Noble Eightfold Path leads to its end, through right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness and concentration. The suttas unfold these truths in the Buddha's own words, through conversations, stories and practice.

- **Vinaya Piṭaka**: the discipline, the rules of life of monks and nuns and the stories behind them.
- **Sutta Piṭaka**: the discourses, the Buddha's teachings to monastics and lay people, the part most people read.
- **Abhidhamma Piṭaka**: the systematic analysis of mind and matter, a later ordering of the teaching.
