# A map of the Pāli Canon

See the relations between the suttas as a map in three dimensions. Each text is a point; the closer two points are, the closer the texts. Rotate the map, zoom in, open a sutta, and read it in the search.

## What you see

Each point is a sutta, named by its reference (MN 10, SN 56.11…) and coloured by its collection. A line between two points is a relation; a distance between two points is a degree of closeness: the suttas that say the same things sit together.

## How to explore it

The map has two views, and the same suttas sit in different places in each:

- **The parallels view** draws the relations established by SuttaCentral: a sutta and its parallels in other collections and languages, the fruit of decades of scholarly work.
- **The meaning view** places each sutta by the meaning of its Pāli text, computed as embeddings: two suttas that speak alike stand close, whether or not anyone had linked them.
- Rotate with the mouse or a finger, zoom with the wheel or a pinch, click a point to see its sutta and its neighbours.

## Where the data comes from

The texts and the parallels come from SuttaCentral. The meaning view is computed on the Pāli text of the Mahāsaṅgīti edition, with the same embeddings the search uses. Nothing is stored about you: the map runs entirely in your browser.
